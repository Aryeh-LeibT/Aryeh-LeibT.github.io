Israel's 26th Knesset: no bloc at 61 in the latest polls

Israel votes on Tuesday 27 October 2026. Surplus-vote agreements are due on 16 October 2026, embassy voting is on 20 October 2026, and official results are expected on 4 November 2026 (single source, not yet confirmed).

Three recent polls, each on its own (seats out of 120): - Channel 12 / Midgam, 12 October 2026 (509 respondents, ±4.4 points): Yashar 23, Likud 20, B'Yachad 13, the Democrats 9, Yisrael Beytenu 9, Otzma Yehudit 8, Shas 8, the Joint List 8, United Torah Judaism 7, Religious Zionism–Zehut 6, Ra'am 5, the Reservists–Economic Party 4. Amcha Yisrael got 1.8% and falls below the threshold. - Maariv / Lazar Research–Panel4All, fieldwork 7–8 October 2026 (700 respondents, ±3.7 points): Yashar 21, Likud 20, B'Yachad 13, Yisrael Beytenu 9, the Democrats 9, Otzma Yehudit 8, Shas 7, UTJ 7, the Joint List 7, Religious Zionism–Zehut 6, Ra'am 5, the Reservists–Economic Party 4, Amcha Yisrael 4. - Walla / Lazar Research, 13 October 2026: Yashar 22, Likud 20, B'Yachad 13, the Democrats 10, with Amcha Yisrael below the threshold (single source, not yet confirmed).

Show the math: blocs. Using each outlet's own bloc grouping, Channel 12 has the parties of the outgoing coalition at 20+8+8+7+6 = 49, the Zionist opposition at 54, the Arab parties at 13, and the Reservists–Economic Party unaligned at 4. Maariv has 52, 52, 12 and 4. Neither main bloc reaches 61 in either poll. The Reservists–Economic Party's 4 seats and the Arab parties' 12–13 are what make every 61 count that people discuss.

Why the threshold dominates. The threshold is 3.25% of valid votes. Because 3.25% of 120 seats is 3.9 seats, the smallest list that passes gets about four, so a list polling 4 is either in with 4 or out with 0. Amcha Yisrael went from 4 seats (Maariv, 9 October 2026) to below the threshold (Channel 12, 12 October 2026) within days. If a list in the bloc falls out, its votes are wasted and every other list's quota goes down. That's why a single list near 3.25% can swing a bloc by four seats on its own.

Surplus agreements. Once the pairs are filed on 16 October 2026, our Show the math panel will run Bader-Ofer with them. A pair can gain a seat that neither list would win alone, but only if both lists pass the threshold.

Poll numbers are seats as published by each pollster, not our projection. We don't average them here.

Show the math

Seats are allocated by the Bader-Ofer method.

  • Threshold: only lists with vi ≥ 0.0325 × Vvalid take part.
  • Quota: q = V / 120, where V is the total votes of the lists that passed the threshold. One seat ≈ q votes.
  • First round: si = floor(vi / q).
  • Remaining seats go one at a time to the list (or surplus-vote pair, counted as one unit) with the highest vi / (si + 1); inside a pair the seats are split again with the same rule.
  • Effective number of parties: N = 1 / Σ pi².
  • Gallagher index: LSq = √( ½ Σ (vi − si)² ), with vote and seat percentages.

Show the math: how the Knesset's 120 seats are divided (Bader-Ofer)

  1. Threshold. Only lists with vi ≥ 0.0325 × Vvalid take part.
  2. Quota. q = Vqualifying / 120, where Vqualifying is the total vote of the lists that passed.
  3. First round. si = floor(vi / q).
  4. Remaining seats. One at a time, to whichever unit has the highest vi / (si + 1). A surplus-vote pair counts as one unit, with its votes and seats combined.
  5. Inside a pair. The pair's seats are split again between the two lists by the same rule. A pair only counts if both lists pass the threshold.

Effective number of parties: N = 1 / Σ pi2, with pi the vote (or seat) share as a fraction.

Gallagher index: LSq = √( ½ Σ (vi − si)2 ), with vi and si as vote and seat percentages. 0 means perfectly proportional.

Worked example (illustrative numbers, not a poll or a result)

Seven lists, 4,005,000 valid votes: A 1,200,000; B 950,000; C 600,000; D 470,000; E 390,000; F 300,000; G 95,000. C and D have a surplus-vote agreement.

  • Threshold: 0.0325 × 4,005,000 = 130,162.5. G (95,000) is out.
  • Vqualifying = 3,910,000, so q = 3,910,000 / 120 = 32,583.33.
  • First round: A 36, B 29, C 18, D 14, E 11, F 9 = 117. Three seats left.
  • Seat 118: E 390,000/12 = 32,500 beats A 1,200,000/37 = 32,432 and C+D 1,070,000/33 = 32,424. E gets it.
  • Seat 119: A 32,432 beats C+D 32,424. A gets it.
  • Seat 120: C+D 32,424 beats B 950,000/30 = 31,667 and A 1,200,000/38 = 31,579. C+D gets it.
  • Inside C+D (33 seats): pair quota 1,070,000/33 = 32,424; C floor(600,000/32,424) = 18, D floor(470,000/32,424) = 14, total 32. Last seat: C 600,000/19 = 31,579 vs D 470,000/15 = 31,333. C gets it.
  • Final: A 37, B 29, C 19, D 14, E 12, F 9, G 0.
  • Without the agreement: seat 120 goes to B (31,667), since C alone has 31,579 and D alone 31,333. Result: B 30, C 18. The agreement moved one seat from B to C.
  • N (votes) = 5.05; N (seats) = 4.81. Gallagher index = 1.92, mostly from G's 2.37% wasted vote.

Rule of thumb for reading polls: one seat ≈ q votes. Every seat figure we project is labelled "Our projection" and comes from these steps.
