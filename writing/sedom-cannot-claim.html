Sedom Cannot Claim to Have Enriched Abraham

Aryeh Leib Thorne Toren · Jerusalem · Friday 23 October 2026 (12 Cheshvan 5787)

The oath in Lech Lecha is not a footnote. It is the condition of the rest of the story.

After the defeat of the four kings, Melech Sedom offers Avram the goods: “Give me the persons, and take the possessions for yourself.” Avram answers with an oath to Hashem El Elyon, Possessor of heaven and earth: “If from a thread to a sandal-strap, or if I take anything that is yours—so that you shall not say, ‘I have made Avram rich.’” He keeps only what his servants have already consumed. He insists that Aner, Eshkol, and Mamre—the men who guarded the baggage—receive their full share. (Bereishit 14:21–24)

Rashi is precise. Hashem has already promised the blessing: “And I will bless you” (12:2). That promise covers wealth. To accept even a thread from Sedom would let the king claim authorship of the enrichment. The raised hand is an oath; the refusal covers both the recovered spoils and any additional reward from Sedom’s treasury. Absolute boundary. Or HaChaim notes that the raised hand allows no partial acceptance, past or future. The Midrash (Bereshit Rabbah 43:9) adds that the thread and the sandal-strap plant later mitzvot for the descendants—tzitzit and chalitzah. The act is not private asceticism. It seeds covenantal institutions.

The sequence is deliberate. First Melchizedek of Shalem blesses Avram in the name of El Elyon and receives a tenth. Gratitude and tithe go to the true Possessor. Only then comes Sedom’s offer. The contrast is structural: honor the source of the blessing, refuse the false claim of enrichment.

This is not isolation. It is non-domination. Avram can later stand before Hashem and argue for the city (18:23–33) precisely because he owes the city nothing. His voice is uncompromised. Lot pitched his tents toward Sedom and became entangled; Avram refuses the thread and remains free to intercede. The covenant path requires that the stand itself not be funded, platformed, or credited by the system one opposes. Otherwise the king can always say, “I enriched you.” Credit stays with the Possessor of heaven and earth. The capacity to do tzedakah u-mishpat remains intact.

Contemporary Abrahams face the same test. Global Sedom is any system—institutional, cultural, economic, or political—that inverts tzedek u-mishpat and then seeks to claim credit for the success or legitimacy of those who stand against it. The thread and the sandal-strap still matter: the grant that softens speech, the platform that requires managed narrative, the alliance that lets the other side own the story of your relevance or prosperity. Accepting them turns Kiddush Hashem into Chilul Hashem. The refusal is not anti-wealth. It is anti-compromise and anti-false-credit. Hashem’s promise is enough.

The fairness remains. Give the baggage-guards their share—the spouses, the colleagues, the quiet workers who held the line while the fight was elsewhere. Refuse the king’s claim. The oath still holds.
