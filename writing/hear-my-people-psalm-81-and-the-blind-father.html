Hear, My People: Psalm 81 and the Blind Father

12 Nov 2026 / 2 Kislev 5787

By Aryeh Leib Thorne Toren

Every Thursday the Levites in the Temple sang Psalm 81. The Mishnah lists the week day by day, and on the fifth day it was "For the leader; on the gittith. Of Asaph" (Mishnah Tamid 7:4). We still say it in shul as the Shir shel Yom.

This Thursday, 12 November 2026, falls in the week of Toldot, and the two texts meet on one sense: hearing.

Yitzchak's eyes were too dim to see. In the tent he touches, he smells, and above all he listens. What he hears doesn't match what he feels: "The voice is the voice of Jacob, yet the hands are the hands of Esau" (Bereishit 27:22). A man who cannot see has to decide by what he hears.

Psalm 81 knows that place. "I heard a language that I knew not" (81:6). Yosef in Egypt heard speech he didn't yet understand. Yitzchak heard a voice he couldn't place. Both had to keep listening past what they already knew.

Then the psalm turns to us: "Hear, My people, and I will admonish you; Israel, if you would but listen to Me!" (81:9). Two verses later: "open your mouth wide and I will fill it" (81:11). The ear comes first, then the mouth.

That's a lesson for anyone who leads. Most of the week we don't see the whole picture. We are asked to listen well before we speak, and to trust a voice we can't yet see.

After Shabbat, Yaakov leaves Beersheba with his father's words in his ears and walks toward Haran. Vayetze begins on the road. Thursday's psalm is a good place to start listening.
