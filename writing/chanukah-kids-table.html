The Story of Chanukah

A long time ago the Greeks ruled the Land of Israel. They would not let Jews learn Torah or keep Shabbat, and they put idols in the Beit HaMikdash, the Holy Temple. Mattityahu and his sons, the Maccabees, were brave and fought for Torah. They won, with Hashem's help.

In the Beit HaMikdash they looked for pure oil to light the Menorah. They found only one small jar, enough for one day. They lit it anyway. Hashem made a miracle, and it burned for eight days. That is why we light for eight nights.

Questions for the Table

  1. The Maccabees lit the oil even though there was only enough for one day. When have you started something even though it looked too big?
  2. Each night we add one candle. What is one good thing you can add each day of Chanukah?
  3. The candles are only for looking at. What do you think they are telling us?
  4. The shamash lights all the other candles. Who helps others "light up" in our family?
  5. We put the menorah in the window. Why do you think we want others to see it?

Games for Younger Kids

Count the Lights. Each night, a child counts the candles out loud, then counts them again to find the new one, the one lit first. How many tonight? How many more until eight?

Find the Shamash. One person hides a small card at the table. Kids look for the "helper." When found, the finder says, "I can help light someone's day."

Spin the Dreidel. The dreidel has four Hebrew letters. After your turn to spin, you take, give, or do nothing. Ask a grown-up for the rules.

One More Light

Every night we add a candle. This week, add one good deed each day: say thank you, help at home, give tzedakah, or call a grandparent.

Friday and Saturday Night

Remember, on Friday 4 and Friday 11 December we light the Chanukah candles BEFORE the Shabbat candles. On Saturday night 5 December we make Havdalah first, then light.
