We Have Surely Seen: Covenant, Memory and Partnership

12 Nov 2026 / 2 Kislev 5787

By Aryeh Leib Thorne Toren

On 11 November 2026, the first of Kislev, the weekly Torah reading reached seven verses that hold almost every question a people faces after a quarrel.

Yitzchak had been sent away from Gerar. He went up to Beersheba, built an altar, pitched a tent and began a new well. Then the king who had sent him away arrived with his councilor and his general, asking for a treaty.

Yitzchak named the grievance in one sentence: "Why have you come to me, seeing that you have been hostile to me and have driven me away from you?"

The king's answer began with a doubled verb. Rashi reads it as two generations of seeing: "We saw it in the case of your father and we see it in the case of yourself."

The two sides still told the story of the expulsion differently. The Torah did not decide between them. The treaty rested on what both had seen.

The same day's learning adds two pictures. The Mishnah in Bechoros 54 says that two flocks too far apart to be counted as one can be joined by animals in the middle, and Shmuel says that one is enough, because "we see the shepherd as one who stands here in the middle." And the Rambam's Laws of Agents and Partners say that a partnership is made when both partners put their money in one pouch and "each of them lifts up the pouch."
