Four Parties, Two Agreements — Surplus Arithmetic After List Lock

29 Elul 5786 · 11 September 2026 · Erev Rosh Hashanah · Israel Politics

I. After the Lists, the Fractions

Party lists for the 26th Knesset are locked. The Central Elections Committee holds the deposited slates; the calendar runs toward embassy voting and election day on 27 October 2026. Mergers that did not happen cannot be reopened. What remains is not inventing a new map of parties, but deciding how votes that clear the 3.25 percent threshold will be converted into seats without needless waste.

This essay stays in the Type 2 lane: cycle facts, named institutional rules, coalition architecture, and the political tests of the weeks ahead. The standing Type 1 frame—limited power under higher law, a people judged by the quality of its common choice—is presupposed, not re-argued. Where theology touches the season, G-d is named that way; a surplus agreement is not teshuvah. It is one of the public instruments by which a free people either practices or evades honest accounting of its ballots.

II. What a Surplus Agreement Is (and Is Not)

Israeli electoral law lets two lists that both clear the threshold pool leftover votes under a surplus agreement (הסכם עודפים). The agreement does not invent seats from parties that die under 3.25 percent. It does not merge lists. It does not move a voter’s ballot from one party to another on election night. It reallocates the fractional remainders that would otherwise sit unused after the first seat allocation—so that, between two partners who both entered the Knesset, fewer votes are arithmetically orphaned.

That is a republican rule announced in advance. It rewards cooperation among lists that have already earned public entry, without rewriting the threshold. After list lock, surplus agreements are one of the few tools that still change seat math without changing who is on the ballot.

Campaigns that sell surplus deals as “guaranteed seats” sell fiction; campaigns that sell them as honest weights for votes cast above the line describe the statute. Times of Israel’s Ariela Karmel (PRIMARY, 10 September 2026) underlines the distinction Netanyahu’s camp tried to blur: these are not the mergers he pushed among smaller right-wing parties at threshold risk. Each of the four opposition lists still runs independently; the pairs only pool surplus when final seats are allocated.

III. Primary Confirmation — ToI, 10 September

PRIMARY — Times of Israel, Ariela Karmel, 10 September 2026 (evening IL). Israel’s four main opposition parties announced Thursday surplus-vote agreements aimed at maximizing the anti-Netanyahu bloc’s representation:

  1. Gadi Eisenkot’s Yashar! will sign with Yair Golan’s Democrats.
  2. Avigdor Liberman’s Yisrael Beiteinu will sign with B’Yachad / Together, the joint list headed by Naftali Bennett and Yair Lapid.

The Democrats, welcoming Yashar!, stated the goal as preventing lost votes, maximizing bloc strength, and replacing “the government responsible for the October 7 failure.” Yashar! said the agreements help ensure that “every ballot cast by Israelis seeking change… will go to one of the parties in the change bloc.”

Government reaction in the same piece: Netanyahu wrote that Eisenkot “will form a left-wing government with Yair Golan, Liberman and the Arab parties”; Smotrich issued a parallel charge. Treat as campaign attribution, not seat arithmetic. The institutional fact is the paired surplus architecture.

ToI also notes Ahmad Tibi’s claim of a Joint List–Ra’am surplus deal—and hedges that neither party had formally announced it. Prefer formal announcements and CEC process. Arab-list surplus talk stays open VERIFY, outside the change-bloc “4 / 2” count.

VI. Civic Virtue and Honest Weights

A republic that announces a threshold and a surplus rule in advance owes matching honesty in how campaigns describe those rules.

  • Limited power: surplus agreements convert seats; they do not declare the opposing Zionist bloc illegitimate.
  • Truth in the gates: prefer ToI / party primaries and CEC process for the deals; named houses with fieldwork dates for polls; Political Arena / Segal / Liel as VERIFY secondary; refuse invented trackers; never Ariel Graph.
  • Equal franchise: the threshold punishes fragmentation without after-the-fact horse-trading over which small list “deserved” seats. Surplus deals are not a backdoor around that rule—or proof that Eisenkot is “really” left merely for pairing remainders with Golan.
  • Shared burden: maximize-against-them messaging and drop-him / keep-him messaging alike will be judged by whether either also speaks shared fate after years of war.

We are on 29 Elul 5786, Erev Rosh Hashanah 5787. Choosing life is a covenantal command; arranging surplus remainders is a civic act under that command. Conflating the two flatters politicians. Separating them keeps the analysis honest.

Fatalism—“the blocs are locked, the deals are theater”—is false here. Lists are locked. Remainders are not. Agency concentrates where the map is closest and wastage is still optional.

VIII. Closing

Post-list-lock Israel on Erev Rosh Hashanah 5787 is change-bloc architecture beside a deadlock poll story. Four change-camp parties. Two surplus agreements at primary-outlet level (ToI, 10 Sep). Segal texture: Eisenkot wanted Liberman, landed with Golan. Lazar/Maariv (9–10 Sep): Yashar 25, Likud 19, Winter over at four, Zionist opposition 58. Winter keep-versus-drop still open on the other flank.

Surplus agreements are the arithmetic of non-wasted votes among lists that clear—not mergers, not polls, not substitutes for turnout. The institutions held the list-lock clock. The parties still hold whether remainders will be weighed in pairs or left on the floor. The people still hold the quality of the choice.

Four parties. Two agreements. That is not paralysis. That is the brief.
