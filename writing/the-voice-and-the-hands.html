The Voice and the Hands

12 Nov 2026 / 2 Kislev 5787

By Aryeh Leib Thorne Toren

On Thursday, 12 November 2026, the second of Kislev 5787, the fifth portion of Toldot reached the oath at Beersheba, the finding of water, and then the tent: a blind father, two sons, and one blessing.

Yitzchak feels him and says the line that has followed the Jewish people ever since: "The voice is the voice of Jacob, yet the hands are the hands of Esau." Rashi hears the difference in the tone. Yaakov spoke "because he speaks in an entreating strain", while "Esau, however, spoke in a harsh strain". The voice of Yaakov is a voice that asks. It is the voice of prayer.

The Hayom Yom for 2 Kislev says prayer needs life, and that you put life into it by giving to a poor man first. The Rebbe Maharash taught: "Davening must be with life." The attached essay, "The Voice and the Hands", gathers the day's portions around those two pictures: the voice that asks gently, and the hand that gives before it asks.
