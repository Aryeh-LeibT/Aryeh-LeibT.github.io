Chanukah Lighting Guide 5787

Aryeh Leib Thorne Toren · Torah scholar, researcher and writer

22 November 2026 · 13 Kislev 5787

Chanukah 2026: first candle Friday 4 December (evening) · eighth candle Friday 11 December (evening)

The Eight Nights

  1. Night 1, Friday 4 December 2026 (24 Kislev): one candle, with all three blessings. Light the menorah BEFORE the Shabbat candles.
  2. Night 2, Saturday night 5 December 2026: two candles, after Shabbat ends and after Havdalah.
  3. Night 3, Sunday 6 December 2026: three candles.
  4. Night 4, Monday 7 December 2026: four candles.
  5. Night 5, Tuesday 8 December 2026: five candles.
  6. Night 6, Wednesday 9 December 2026: six candles.
  7. Night 7, Thursday 10 December 2026: seven candles.
  8. Night 8, Friday 11 December 2026 (1 Tevet 5787): eight candles, before the Shabbat candles.

Blessings 1 and 2 every night. Blessing 3 only on the first night, or the first time you light this year.

How to Light

Set the candles in a straight row, all the same height, with the shamash (helper candle) apart from the row. Place them from right to left: night 1 on the right, and add each new one to the left of the others. Light the newest candle first, then go left to right. Say the blessings first, then light. The lights are only for looking at, so do not use them to read or work by.

Friday and Saturday Night

On Friday, light the Chanukah candles early, before the Shabbat candles, with larger candles or extra oil so they burn well into the night. After Shabbat lights are lit, do not relight or move the menorah. On Saturday night, say Havdalah first, then light the menorah. This is the order in the Chabad.org guide.

The Blessings (in English letters)

  1. Baruch atah Hashem, our G-d, melech ha'olam, asher kidshanu b'mitzvotav v'tzivanu l'hadlik ner Chanukah.
  2. Baruch atah Hashem, our G-d, melech ha'olam, she'asah nisim la'avoteinu bayamim hahem, bazman hazeh.
  3. (First night only.) Baruch atah Hashem, our G-d, melech ha'olam, shehecheyanu v'kiyemanu v'higi'anu lazman hazeh.

In English: Blessed are You, Hashem our G-d, King of the universe, who has made us holy with His commandments and commanded us to light the Chanukah light; who made miracles for our fathers in those days, at this time; who has given us life, sustained us and brought us to this time.

Good to Know

Light after nightfall or shortly after sunset, with enough oil or candle to burn for half an hour after nightfall. Place the menorah in a window or doorway so that others see it. Check your local candle-lighting times at Chabad.org, because Friday times change by city.
