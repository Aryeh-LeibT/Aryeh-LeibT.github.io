The Flame of Yosef

Aryeh Leib Thorne Toren · Torah scholar, researcher and writer

Prepared Sunday 22 November 2026 (13 Kislev 5787)

Vayeshev week, from Sunday 29 November to Shabbat 5 December 2026 (19 to 25 Kislev 5787): Yud-Tes Kislev, the Chitas, and the first day of Chanukah

Bereishit 37:1 to 40:23 · Maftir Bamidbar 7:1 to 17 · Haftarah Zechariah 2:14 to 4:7 · Tehillim 90 to 119

Preface

This booklet is for the week of Parshat Vayeshev in the Chabad daily cycle. It runs from Sunday, 29 November 2026 (19 Kislev 5787), Yud-Tes Kislev, when the daily Chumash opens Vayeshev, to Shabbat, 5 December 2026 (25 Kislev 5787), when the whole parsha is read in shul. That Shabbat is also the first day of Chanukah. The first light is kindled on Friday afternoon, 4 December 2026, before the Shabbat candles.

A note on the date. The Shabbat that follows Vayishlach is 5 December, not 28 November. The Torah reading for Shabbat, 28 November 2026 (18 Kislev 5787), is still Vayishlach. Hebcal’s Torah reading data for 28 November 2026 gives Genesis 32:4 to 36:43, and its data for 5 December gives Genesis 37:1 to 40:23. So this booklet is built for the week that begins on Yud-Tes Kislev and ends on Shabbat Chanukah.

The haftarah this year is not the usual one. The ordinary haftarah of Vayeshev is Amos 2:6 to 3:8, and that is what Chabad.org’s Haftorah in a Nutshell gives. But when Vayeshev falls on Shabbat Chanukah, Chabad.org gives Zechariah 2:14 to 4:7, because that passage holds the vision of the golden menorah. The Chabad.org page for this exact Shabbat, 5 December 2026, lists Zechariah 2:14 to 4:7 and a maftir of Numbers 7:1 to 17. This booklet gives both haftarot in full, and says which one is read.

Why it was made. The sources are the Chumash and Rashi as printed on Sefaria, the Chabad.org daily study and calendar pages, Chabad.org’s adaptations of the Rebbe’s sichos, and a few other works named in the bibliography. Each borrowed teaching is credited to its author or its publication.

How it is built. Part One gives the week at a glance. Part Two connects Vayishlach, which we have just read, to Vayeshev. Part Three goes through the parsha one aliyah a day, the way the Chabad daily Chumash is learned: a short overview, a few comments from Rashi, one teaching from the Rebbe where the sources give one, and then the full text of that day’s verses in Hebrew and English. Part Four is the maftir and the haftarot. Part Five is the week’s Tehillim, Tanya and a Yud-Tes Kislev and Chanukah reading. Part Six is for the Shabbat table. The back matter has a list of what is confirmed and what rests on one source, the key psalms of the week in full, a glossary and the bibliography.

On the texts. The Hebrew verses are from the Sefaria edition “Miqra according to the Masorah”, printed here without vowels or cantillation. The English is the JPS Tanakh (1985) as hosted on Sefaria. In both, the Names of G-d are written in the forms used in this house: Hashem and G-d in English, and ה' and אלקים in Hebrew, with the other Names broken by a hyphen. Rashi in English follows the Rosenbaum and Silbermann translation on Sefaria, shortened and often paraphrased.

On what is not settled. A few items rest on a single source or could not be checked against the original; the text says so where they appear, and Appendix A lists them. The Tehillim links in Chapter 13 are suggestions for reflection.
