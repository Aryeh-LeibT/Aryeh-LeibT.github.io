The Storm-Tossed Banner: Aniyah So’arah as the Seed of Sapphire Foundations

7 August 2026 · Aryeh Leib Thorne Toren

Originally written 7 August 2026 (Haftarah of Re'eh, read 8 August 2026 / 25 Av 5786). Archive: Haftarah · Shiva d'Nechemta.

The aniyah so’arah lo nuchamah — the afflicted, storm-tossed Zion who still refuses partial comfort — is no cry of despair but the precise measure of hester panim whose calibrated depth becomes the womb of gilui. Every tempest of exile, every “small moment” of concealment from the Churban through later catastrophes, seeds the sapphire foundations, the limudei Hashem who are also the bonayikh builders, the inviolable heritage that “no weapon formed against thee shall prosper,” and the covenantal republican sovereignty grounded in Torah. The rebuke of history is the seed of greater light; venahafoch hu is already underway in the city whose stones are being set with fair colors even as the storm continues to reveal its purpose.

Liturgical and Calendrical Setting

We stand in the heart of the Sheva d’Nechemta, the seven weeks of consolation that follow Tisha B’Av. The progression is not a simple ascent of comfort but a living dialogue. First Hashem commands the prophets: Nachamu nachamu ami. Zion answers in the second week: Vatomer Tzion azavani Hashem. In this third week the complaint itself is voiced: Aniyah so’arah lo nuchamah — the storm-tossed one remains uncomforted by intermediaries. Only later will the direct divine reply come: Anochi Anochi hu menachemchem. The order is exact, as the Abudraham and Levush teach: this is a living exchange between the Holy One and Knesset Yisrael. The uncomforted cry is not regression; it is the necessary arousal from below that draws the fuller gilui from Above.

This Shabbat is also Mevarchim Chodesh Elul, the final blessing of a new month. We stand between the destruction commemorated in Av and the intense teshuvah of Elul, between the storm and the sapphire. Parashat Re’eh itself opens with the command to see: “See, I set before you today blessing and curse.” The vision demanded by the parsha is the same vision that refuses partial consolation in the Haftarah. We are asked not merely to hear the prophets but to see the divine reality itself — and anything less feels like continued storm.

Textual and Traditional Reconnaissance

The prophet addresses Zion as a woman still battered by the gale:

עֲנִיָּה סֹעֲרָה לֹא נֻחָמָה

“O afflicted, storm-tossed one, uncomforted!”

Rashi hears in so’arah the heart that storms with many troubles. Radak locates the storm in the long exile itself. Malbim notes that even after the ingathering of children (the theme of the previous consolation), Zion remains uncomforted because the Temple is not yet rebuilt on its hill. The physical and spiritual restoration must still be completed.

Then the promise:

הִנֵּה אָנֹכִי מַרְבִּיץ בַּפּוּךְ אֲבָנַיִךְ וִיסַדְתִּיךְ בַּסַּפִּירִים

“Behold, I will set your stones in antimony and lay your foundations with sapphires.”

Battlements of rubies, gates of precious stones, the entire enclosing wall of gems. The city that was storm-tossed will be rebuilt in materials that catch and refract light — materials that themselves suggest sefirah, the luminous channels of divine revelation. Malbim reads the precious stones as corresponding to the tribes and their virtues: sapphire for the clarity of Torah (Yissachar), diamonds for leadership (Yehudah). The rebuilding is not merely aesthetic; it is the externalization of inner righteousness.

Immediately the spiritual core is revealed:

וְכָל־בָּנַיִךְ לִמּוּדֵי ה' וְרַב שְׁלוֹם בָּנָיִךְ

“And all your children shall be disciples of the Lord, and great shall be the peace of your children.”

The Talmud (Berachot 64a) famously reads banayikh as bonayikh — your builders. Torah scholars increase peace in the world. Metzudat David explains that the children will excel in wisdom as if they were the direct pupils of the Holy One. Righteousness will establish the city; oppression and ruin will be kept far; no weapon formed against her shall succeed; every tongue that rises in judgment shall be condemned. This is the heritage of the servants of Hashem. Malbim’s dual reading of the weapons — physical force and intellectual or ideological assault — remains relevant: both seek to sever the people from emunah; neither shall prosper when the foundations are sapphire and the children are limudei Hashem.

The second movement of the Haftarah opens the invitation:

הוֹי כָּל־צָמֵא לְכוּ לַמַּיִם

“Ho, all who are thirsty, come for water… buy wine and milk without money and without price.”

Why spend for that which is not bread? The classical commentaries identify the water, wine, and milk with Torah. Radak and Ibn Ezra hear the call addressed also to the nations who will run to Israel in the end of days. Rashi and others hear it first as a summons to Israel itself: the Torah is free, available, and alone satisfies. An everlasting covenant is promised — the enduring loyalties of David — and nations that did not know you shall run to you because the Holy One of Israel has glorified you.

Chassidic Deepening: The Hester–Gilui Axis

The storm is not random. Hester panim is purposeful and calibrated. The depth of the concealment measures the intensity of the revelation that will follow. The so’arah that still refuses comfort is the soul’s refusal to settle for anything less than direct divine presence. In the language of Tanya and later Chassidus, the exile is the descent that makes possible a lower unity more profound than the original higher unity. The most intense hester of modern times and the subsequent emergence of Jewish sovereignty and continued ingathering form corresponding movements of gilui. The “small moment” of affliction is the seed of the greater light. Sapphire foundations are not merely beautiful; they are the crystallisation of clarity that can only emerge after the storm has done its work of purification.

Teachings associated with the sequence of Va’etchanan–Eikev–Re’eh and their Haftarot emphasize that the highest service is not merely to hear the prophets but to see G-dliness itself. The cry of aniyah so’arah is the expression of that dissatisfaction with intermediate consolation. Hashem answers by promising a rebuilding whose very materials — sapphire, ruby, carbuncle — are vehicles of light.

Venahafoch Hu and Historical Reversal

The entire movement of the Haftarah is venahafoch hu — the Purim paradigm extended across history. From storm-tossed to jewel-encrusted. From spiritual and material poverty to free waters of Torah. From oppression and fear to inviolable security. From the Davidic line that seemed broken to an everlasting covenant in which Israel becomes a prince and commander of peoples, and nations run toward the glory of the Holy One of Israel.

Historical experience has embodied chapters of this reversal: from destruction and exile to sovereignty and the flourishing of Torah study. The tongue that contended against the people in the courts of the nations has not ceased, yet the weapon formed against them has not prospered as the designers intended. The reversal is not complete — the Temple is not yet rebuilt, the storm still howls in places — but the direction is unmistakable. The concealment continues to reveal its purpose in the sovereignty that is inhabited, transmitted, and defended.

Covenantal Republican Dimensions

Jewish sovereignty is itself a political gilui after long hester. The city established through righteousness (b’tzedakah tikkoneni) is the covenantal republic in miniature: liberty under Torah, active virtue as hishtadlut balanced by bitachon, non-domination (no oppression shall come near), and institutions that serve the common good rather than arbitrary power. The limudei Hashem who are also bonayikh are the living constitution of that republic — scholars and builders whose learning increases peace.

In the language of covenantal republicanism, the people under Torah are neither subjects of a tyrant nor atomised individuals under an abstract universalism. They are free persons bound by covenant, exercising self-rule in the light of a higher law, transmitting that law to the next generation, and refusing to dominate or be dominated. The promise that no weapon formed against them shall succeed is not magical immunity; it is the security that flows from living inside the covenant while engaging in the hishtadlut of defense, diplomacy, and moral clarity. The nations that run to Israel do so because they recognise a glory that is not imperial but revelatory.

Daniel J. Elazar’s analysis of the Jewish covenantal tradition supplies a conceptual matrix: the edah as a republic of partners under a higher constitution (Torah), a federal matrix of interlocking covenants rather than a unitary state-over-people. Philip Pettit’s neo-republican freedom as non-domination maps onto the promise that oppression shall not come near. Eric Nelson’s study of the Hebrew Republic demonstrates the historical influence of rabbinic and biblical political thought upon early modern European republicanism. The dual jurisdictional model articulated by Rabbeinu Nissim of Gerona (the Ran) and the anti-monarchical readings of Abravanel supply indigenous Jewish resources for limited government and institutional checks.

Critical evaluation is necessary. Pure historicist readings of Second Isaiah risk reducing a living prophetic trajectory to a sixth-century literary artifact. Pure liberal contractualism remains thinner than the biblical brit, lacking the vertical dimension of divine partnership and the mechanisms of memory and transmission. Pure statist realism treats sovereignty as mere power calculus and forgets the moral and educational preconditions of endurance. The synthesis of Orthodox theology with covenantal republican theory avoids these reductions: it grounds non-domination in subordination to higher law, locates civic virtue in the formation of limudei Hashem who are also bonayikh, and understands the free waters of Torah as the public good upon which the republic depends.

Educational Application

The conversation at the Shabbat table can move on two levels.

For younger children: Hashem is rebuilding His city with the most beautiful shining stones — sapphires and rubies — because He loves us. When we learn Torah and do kindness, we help put those shining stones in place. Children become those who learn directly from Hashem and bring great peace.

For older students and adults: We are raising limudei Hashem who are also bonayikh. Daily Torah study is the resilience protocol that keeps the heart from being entirely storm-tossed. Historical catastrophe must be told not as the final word but as the intense hester whose depth seeded sovereignty and the flourishing of Torah learning. Every act of education that forms a person who can see both the storm and the sapphire is zikui ha-rabbim — bringing merit to the many by transmitting the living standard. The task is not merely to survive the gale but to raise builders of the next stage of gilui.

Questions for discussion:

  • What does it mean that the children are both disciples and builders?
  • Where do we still feel “uncomforted,” and how might that feeling itself be a form of hishtadlut that draws greater revelation?
  • How does the free water of Torah differ from knowledge that costs money yet does not satisfy?
  • In what ways does the promise that “no weapon formed against thee shall prosper” require both bitachon and active hishtadlut?

Closing

The storm-tossed, still-uncomforted Zion of this third consolation is the calibrated depth of hester panim whose very measure seeds the sapphire foundations of gilui, the limudei Hashem who build and defend, the venahafoch hu from affliction to covenantal republican liberty under Torah, and the education that raises the next generation as living standards of that light.

The concealment continues to reveal its purpose in the sovereignty that is inhabited, transmitted, and defended.

Chazak v’nitchazak.
