A Public Covenant for the Classroom — The Jewish Students’ Bill of Rights

29 Elul 5786 · Friday, 11 September 2026 (Erev RH 5787)

The High Holidays have landed inside the first weeks of school. That is not a metaphor. In most North American districts, Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur arrive while lockers are still being claimed, schedules still settling, and “community norms” still being taught as if they were weather. For Jewish students, the calendar and the corridor now share a season.

Into that overlap, five major American Jewish organizations released a Jewish Students’ Bill of Rights for K-12: Jewish Federations of North America (JFNA), the Anti-Defamation League (ADL), American Jewish Committee (AJC), the Conference of Presidents of Major American Jewish Organizations, and StandWithUs. [VERIFY: AJC 9 Sep 2026; JPost 10 Sep 2026.] The document is not a statute. It is not a court order. It is a public covenant proposed to schools and districts — six baseline standards that parents and communities can hold up and ask: do you do this, or do you not?

That question matters more than the press conference. Announcement is not adoption. A manifesto that sits in a PDF is not a policy that sits in a handbook. Hold that hedge from the first paragraph, or the essay collapses into cheerleading.

The numbers that refuse to flatter

The coalition’s timing is not only liturgical. It is empirical.

ADL’s most recent Audit of Antisemitic Incidents found that overall antisemitic incidents nationwide declined by roughly a third in 2025 — about 33%. That is the headline people want to celebrate. The K-12 line under it is colder: incidents at non-Jewish elementary and secondary schools stayed near flat — 825 in 2025 against 860 in 2024. [VERIFY: ADL Audit figures as cited by AJC / JFNA / JPost secondary; do not invent further breakdowns.]

So the national curve bent down. The school corridor largely did not. That is the gap the Bill of Rights is written to name. Not “everything is worse everywhere.” Not “everything is fine now.” A residual hardness where children spend their days — which is exactly where belonging either becomes practice or becomes theatre.

G-d’s name does not rubber-stamp a press release. It does demand that we refuse both panic and complacency. A one-third national decline is real news. A near-flat K-12 count is also real news. Hold both.

Six standards — infrastructure, not vibes

The Bill lays out six concrete standards. Paraphrased carefully from the coalition text: [VERIFY exact wording against AJC / JFNA / StandWithUs PDF before public reuse.]

  1. Train the adults. Administrators and staff must be equipped to identify antisemitism as it actually appears in modern educational settings — not only as a museum exhibit from another century.
  2. Transparent reporting. Clear processes for reporting and responding to antisemitic incidents, under applicable law, with the same gravity given to other hate and bias incidents — plus accountability and follow-through.
  3. Accurate teaching. Instruction about Jewish culture, diversity, history, the Holocaust, antisemitism, and Israel must be accurate and representative — not a caricature, not a campaign.
  4. Accommodate observance. Treat religious observances as excused absences; avoid scheduling major school events on widely celebrated Jewish holidays.
  5. Protect identity — including Israel connection — without coercion. Jewish students may express identity and participate fully without religious coercion, bullying, or pressure to distance themselves from any part of that identity, including connection to Israel.
  6. Balanced contested politics. Educators and curricula present contested issues in a balanced way rather than using the classroom to advance a specific political or ideological viewpoint.

Read them again without the branding. Training. Process. Accuracy. Calendar dignity. Non-coercion of identity. Epistemic balance in the classroom. That is not a party platform. It is antisemitism infrastructure — the dull, necessary machinery of equal standing.

Fingerhut’s line, as reported, lands the non-special-rights frame cleanly: schools are not being asked for special treatment; they are being asked to give Jewish students the same seriousness and protection every other student in the building already receives. [VERIFY quote attribution to Eric Fingerhut / JFNA.] Greenblatt’s warning cuts the other way: when a school cannot identify or respond to antisemitism, the lesson every Jewish child absorbs is that they do not belong. [VERIFY quote attribution to Jonathan Greenblatt / ADL.] Daroff and Rothstein, as reported, press the same adult-responsibility point: the weight of educating the room about Jew-hatred should not fall on the child in the desk. [VERIFY: Conference of Presidents / StandWithUs statements via AJC–JFNA releases.]

Covenantal republican texture, lightly applied: non-domination. A student who must hide a Magen David, skip saying “Israel,” or become the unpaid diversity trainer for the class is already living under a soft domination — even when no one throws a punch. Soft domination is still domination. Infrastructure is how a free people answers it without turning every disagreement into a show trial.

Announce ≠ adopt — the VERIFY hinge

Here is the hinge this essay will not blur.

The coalition released a Bill of Rights. That is a verified public fact as of 9–10 September 2026. What is not verified — and must not be claimed — is wholesale district adoption, board votes, handbook revisions, or training rollouts. Announce ≠ compliance. A PDF is not a policy. A joint press line is not a superintendent’s signature. [VERIFY hedge: track local federation / district responses separately; do not name-attack individual superintendents.]

Why insist? Because Diaspora politics loves the victory lap. Because Israel–Diaspora commentary loves the institutional photo. Because parents need usable standards, not another round of “major groups said.” The honest work after a Bill of Rights is boring: school-board packets, handbook language, incident logs that close, staff who can tell contemporary antisemitism from “I disagree with Netanyahu.” Cheer the standards. Measure the adoption. Do not confuse them.

JFNA’s page also lists Canada’s Centre for Israel and Jewish Affairs (CIJA) among releasers; AJC and JPost lead with the five U.S. organizations named above. [VERIFY coalition membership carefully by outlet — do not flatten.] Either way, the six standards are the substance. Membership footnotes do not change the announce/adopt gap.

Israel connection without coercion — peoplehood, not a loyalty oath

Standard five will draw the most heat, and it should be read carefully.

Protecting a Jewish student’s right to express identity including connection to Israel, without pressure to distance from it, is not a demand that every classmate become a Zionist. It is a refusal of the classroom as a coercion chamber. Peoplehood is not an extracurricular. For vast numbers of Jewish families, Israel is not a foreign-policy hobby; it is grandmother’s Hebrew, a cousin’s army service, a prayer for Jerusalem, a summer visit, a newspaper on the kitchen table. To tell a child that belonging at school requires amputating that strand is not “neutrality.” It is a demand for partial self-erasure.

The Bill’s own balance clause (standard six) cuts both ways. Contested politics belong in the classroom as contested — taught with accuracy, not as a single ideological campaign. A school that cannot distinguish critique of Israeli policy from harassment of Jewish kids has already failed the training standard. A school that treats Jewish Israel-connection as uniquely radioactive while other diasporas keep their peoplehood intact has already failed equal dignity.

This essay refuses two cheap moves. It refuses to claim that every classroom slight is 1938. It refuses to claim that “just politics” excuses bullying a child for a flag, a name, a holiday, or a people. G-d’s brit does not baptise every Israeli cabinet decision. Neither does it license turning Jewish peoplehood into the one identity a student must learn to mute.

High Holidays in the first weeks — calendar as equal standing

Standard four looks small until you live it. Excused absences for religious observance. No major events parked on widely celebrated Jewish holidays. In a year when the Days of Awe sit inside the opening weeks, that is not a niche request. It is whether the school calendar can see Jews as Jews without treating them as a scheduling inconvenience.

Elul closing into Rosh Hashanah is already a season of accounting. Parents are counting something else too: will the district treat Yom Kippur as real, or as optional? Will the assembly land on a fast day? Will the Jewish kid become the problem because the calendar refused to look up? Equal standing starts in the planner before it starts in the DEI slide deck.

What this is for — and what it refuses

For: a public covenant parents can cite; a checklist federations and school partners can train to; a shared language across AJC’s education advocacy, ADL’s audit and training capacity, StandWithUs’s K-12 fairness work, Federation community leverage, and Conference of Presidents communal standing. [VERIFY resource hubs / toolkits via AJC Center for Education Advocacy and StandWithUs download page — do not invent program metrics.]

Refuses: inventing district adoption counts; naming and shaming individual superintendents; flattening every Israel debate into antisemitism; flattening every antisemitism claim into “just criticism”; turning High Holiday anxiety into content; posting school-politics rage bait without Aryeh’s yes.

Prefer primary and careful secondary (AJC, JFNA, JPost, StandWithUs). Prefer CEC if any later Israel-electoral foil appears in adjacent files — never Ariel Graph. This essay does not need electoral graphs; keep them out.

Practical hishtadlut — watch list

  1. Read the Bill, not only the headline. Hold schools to the six standards as written. [VERIFY: StandWithUs download / AJC full text.]
  2. Separate announce from adopt. Ask your district: which of the six are already in the handbook, which are in training this semester, which are aspirational?
  3. Incident process first. Transparent reporting with follow-through beats another assembly slogan.
  4. Curriculum accuracy. Holocaust, Jewish history, antisemitism, and Israel — accurate and representative; contested politics balanced, not weaponised.
  5. Calendar dignity this month. Excused observance; no major events on the High Holidays where avoidable.
  6. Identity without coercion. Connection to Israel may be expressed; bullying and forced distancing are out — without turning the classroom into a loyalty court.

Bottom line

Overall antisemitic incidents fell hard in 2025; non-Jewish K-12 did not follow — 825 versus 860, near flat. Into the High Holidays’ collision with the first weeks of school, JFNA, ADL, AJC, the Conference of Presidents, and StandWithUs offered a six-point public covenant: train staff on contemporary antisemitism; report transparently; teach accurately; accommodate observance; protect Jewish identity and Israel connection without coercion; keep contested politics balanced. That is antisemitism infrastructure and equal standing — not special rights, not a victory lap.

The VERIFY hinge stands: announce ≠ district adoption. Measure handbooks, trainings, and closed incident logs. Keep G-d’s name as reverence, not branding. Hold the publish button on school politics until Aryeh says yes. The classroom is where Diaspora Jewish children learn whether peoplehood is permitted in public — or only in private, after the bell.
