Chapter 2. The Haftarah in Brief

How the haftarah answers the parsha. Three links stand out when the two are read side by side. These are mine, drawn from the verses and from Rashi.

  • The flight and the service, in one verse. “Then Yaakov had to flee to the land of Aram; there Israel served for a wife, for a wife he had to guard sheep” (12:13). That is Parshat Vayetze in a line: the flight from Be’er Sheva (Bereishit 28:10) and the years of service for Rachel (29:18).
  • Who did the guarding. Rashi on 12:13 hears Hashem speaking: when Yaakov was forced to flee, “you know how I guarded him.” Radak on the same verse says it plainly in Hebrew: they do not remember the good I did for their father, who fled from Esav, served Lavan for a wife and guarded his flock, “and I was the One who was with him and blessed him, and he returned from there with wealth.” That is the promise at the ladder kept: “I am with you: I will protect you wherever you go” (Bereishit 28:15), where the Hebrew word for “protect” is וּשְׁמַרְתִּיךָ, “I will guard you.”
  • The angel and the heel. “In the womb he tried to supplant his brother; grown to manhood, he strove with a divine being, he strove with an angel and prevailed” (12:4 to 5). The haftarah reaches back to Toldot and forward to Vayishlach, with Beit El between: “at Bethel [Yaakov] would meet him, there to commune with him.”

Chapter 6. The Word “Guard”: A Thread Through the Parsha, the Haftarah and the Psalm

This chapter is my own reading. No source I know of joins all of these verses. I offer it as a way into the week, not as a teaching of Chazal.

The root שמר, “guard,” runs through all three texts in this printable. Here are the verses, in order.

והנה אנכי עמך ושמרתיך בכל אשר-תלך והשבתיך אל-האדמה הזאת כי לא אעזבך עד אשר אם-עשיתי את אשר-דברתי לך

28:15 Remember, I am with you: I will protect you wherever you go and will bring you back to this land. I will not leave you until I have done what I have promised you.”

At the ladder, Hashem promises to guard Yaakov wherever he goes. The JPS says “protect”; the Hebrew is וּשְׁמַרְתִּיךָ, from the root שמר.

וידר יעקב נדר לאמר אם-יהיה אלקים עמדי ושמרני בדרך הזה אשר אנכי הולך ונתן-לי לחם לאכל ובגד ללבש

28:20 Jacob then made a vow, saying, “If G-d remains with me, if He protects me on this journey that I am making, and gives me bread to eat and clothing to wear,

ושבתי בשלום אל-בית אבי והיה ה' לי לאלקים

28:21 and if I return safe to my father’s house—Hashem shall be my G-d.

On the road, Yaakov asks for that guarding, “if He protects me on this journey,” where again the Hebrew is וּשְׁמָרַנִי, “and guards me.”

ויאמר מה אתן-לך ויאמר יעקב לא-תתן-לי מאומה אם-תעשה-לי הדבר הזה אשובה ארעה צאנך אשמר

30:31 He said, “What shall I pay you?” And Jacob said, “Pay me nothing! If you will do this thing for me, I will again pasture and keep your flocks:

In Charan, after fourteen years, Yaakov is the one who guards: “I will again pasture and keep your flocks,” where “keep” is אֶשְׁמֹר, “I will guard.”

ויברח יעקב שדה ארם ויעבד ישראל באשה ובאשה שמר

12:13 Then Jacob had to flee to the land of Aram; There Israel served for a wife, For a wife he had to guard [sheep].

ובנביא העלה ה' את-ישראל ממצרים ובנביא נשמר

12:14 But when Hashem Brought Israel up from Egypt, It was through a prophet; Through a prophet they were guarded.

Hoshea says it twice. “For a wife he guarded,” Yaakov guarding sheep. And “through a prophet they were guarded,” Israel guarded by Moshe. Rashi on 12:13 hears in the first a hint of the second: you know how I guarded him.

And the psalm ends on it: “Hashem will guard your going and coming, now and forever” (Tehillim 121:8), which the Midrash reads as “And Yaakov went out.”

The thread, in a few lines. A man goes out alone at sunset and is promised that he will be guarded. He asks to be guarded on the way. He spends twenty years guarding another man’s sheep. The prophet, centuries later, tells his children that their father guarded sheep for a wife, and that they too were guarded, through a prophet. And the psalm that the Midrash puts in Yaakov’s mouth says that the One who guards Israel neither slumbers nor sleeps. The one who guards is guarded. That is my reading, offered for the Shabbat table.
