Wells, Witnesses and Heirs: An Expanded Edition

12 Nov 2026 / 2 Kislev 5787

By Aryeh Leib Thorne Toren

On 10 November 2026 I finished a second, expanded edition of Wells, Witnesses and Heirs. The first edition was 66 pages and five chapters, drawn from Chayei Sarah, Toldot and Tehillim 144. This edition runs to 98 pages. It adds a new introduction, fuller source sections, and a new sixth chapter on the two haftaros of those weeks, An Old King and Two Brothers.

This is a different booklet from Wells, Psalms and the New Knesset, which was posted on LinkedIn on 10 November 2026. That one gathered the fifty topics of 30 Cheshvan. This one stays with three words: wells, witnesses and heirs.

The downloadable excerpt is the introduction and Chapter Four, Wells by Their Old Names. In Toldot, read on Shabbos, 14 November 2026, Yitzchak digs again the wells his father had dug, and "he gave them the same names that his father had given them" (Bereishis 26:18, in the 1985 Jewish Publication Society translation). The Midrash, reading why the Pelishtim stopped those wells after Avraham's death, says that a great man holds things in place, and that Hashem raises up someone to stand in his place. Yitzchak kept the old names because he was not founding something new. He was the one raised up so that what his father built would not slip away.

The other five chapters are in the 98-page edition.
