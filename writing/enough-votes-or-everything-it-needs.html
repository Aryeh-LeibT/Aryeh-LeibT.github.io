Enough Votes, or Everything It Needs

Israel politics, read through the fourth reading of Vayishlach, the daily section for 25 November 2026 (Genesis 33)

Aryeh Leib Thorne Toren

26 November 2026 · 16 Kislev 5787 · Jerusalem · in the week of Parashas Vayishlach (Shabbat 28 November 2026)

Prepared in deep partnership and cooperation with Grok and xAI

Full disclosure: I have a bias. I voted for the Religious Zionist Party.

Update, 26 November 2026: On Wednesday, 25 November, Liberman said the talks with Yashar! had been concluded and needed only a formal document; Channel 13 reported that evening that the deal would give Yisrael Beytenu the Defense, Justice, and Aliyah and Integration ministries; and the Knesset passed, by a vote of 65 to 48, the preliminary reading of a bill that would bar an indicted member of Knesset from being tasked with forming a government.

In the fourth reading of Vayishlach, the daily section for 25 November 2026, Yaakov and Esau each say they have enough, in different words.

Esau says, "I have enough, my brother." His word is rav. Yaakov answers yesh li kol, I have everything. Rashi draws the line: Yaakov means "all that will supply my needs. But Esau spoke proudly," saying he had abundance, "far more than my needs."

Rav is a pile. Kol is a fit. One measures what you have against other people. The other measures it against the job in front of you.

That is Israel's question this week.

Gadi Eisenkot holds the mandate to form a government, and on Tuesday evening, 24 November, his Yashar! party announced its first coalition partner: he and Avigdor Liberman agreed to sign a coalition agreement between Yashar! and Yisrael Beytenu. The announcement kept the terms confidential.

The same evening, Yair Golan's Democrats suspended their talks with Yashar!, protesting its parallel unity talks with Likud. Those talks have produced preliminary understandings, including a ministerial committee to work out agreed changes to the justice system. The Yashar! and Likud teams met for a fourth round on Wednesday, 25 November. On Thursday, 26 November, Yashar! and the Democrats announced in a joint statement that they had agreed to sign a coalition deal, with some details still to be finalized. The Democrats are to receive four ministerial portfolios, among them an expanded internal security portfolio. They did not get the Justice or Education ministries they had sought.

Meanwhile United Torah Judaism received rabbinical approval to advance talks with Eisenkot's camp. The Jerusalem Post stressed that the approval covers negotiations only, not joining the government or supporting its swearing-in. The outline under discussion, as several outlets describe it, drops enlistment quotas, lets full-time Torah learners keep learning, and pairs that with a call from the leading rabbis for young men who are not learning to enlist, in supervised Haredi units. On the evening of 25 November, Walla and the Jerusalem Post reported a breakthrough: a further understanding to keep the religion-and-state status quo, with a source saying about 75 percent of the details were settled. On the morning of 26 November, Channel 12's Amit Segal reported that a decision was still pending from Rabbi Mordechai Hirsch.

So Eisenkot has had several doors open: unity with Likud, a Haredi deal, and, the Jerusalem Post reported on 24 November, frameworks for a government sworn in through other parties' abstention. Two of them are narrower than they were. On 25 November Liberman told Kan Reshet Bet, "There will be no minority government; there will be a government with a Jewish majority." And on 26 November Walla reported that senior Likud officials believe the prospects of a unity government are diminishing, with Amir Ohana saying, "I think the most likely outcome is that they will form a government without us."

Thursday brought more. Liberman wrote on 26 November that "the government of change is taking shape." The same day Naftali Bennett's party said that "under no circumstances" will it join the Netanyahu bloc, and that the best option "by far" is a Zionist unity government headed first by Eisenkot. Ra'am's Mansour Abbas predicted a government of the change bloc, United Torah Judaism and Ra'am, with the Joint List as a parliamentary safety net. JFeed reported that Yashar! answered, "We're not even talking to them."

Every door that stays open can produce rav: enough hands, or enough absent hands, to pass a confidence vote. The harder question is which one produces kol.

What does an Israeli government need right now? Full ranks in an army still at war. A budget. One voice on Iran, next to an American president who is setting deadlines. On Monday, 23 November, Eisenkot told his own faction, "There will be no compromise on establishing a state commission of inquiry or on military service for all." Whatever he signs, and every partner's demands, should be measured against those words.

A government that exists only because enough people agreed not to vote against it has rav. It may not have kol.

Yaakov's enough was sized to his mission. Israel's next government should be sized the same way: not to the smallest number that survives a vote, but to the work that is waiting for it.
