Not to Another Place

12 Nov 2026 / 2 Kislev 5787

By Aryeh Leib Thorne Toren

On 12 November 2026, Iran's security chief, Mohsen Rezaei, drew a line around one thing. It was not a ship or a strait. It was a pile of enriched uranium.

Speaking to Al Mayadeen on 11 November, he said Iran "will not remove a single atom of enriched nuclear material", and that Tehran had already told Moscow. He gave a reason: Iran needs the material "to design ships and submarines that work on nuclear energy". That is his stated reason, not a settled technical fact. Naval reactors can run on low-enriched uranium.

The same day's learning, in the Rambam's Laws of Agents and Partners, has a ruling that fits. One partner wants to take the shared goods to another city. The other may refuse: "I do not desire to give you the money that is in my possession and then have to pursue you and bring you to court to expropriate it from you". A promise is not the same as goods you can see.

In the nuclear talks, each side is that second partner. The attached essay is "Not to Another Place".
