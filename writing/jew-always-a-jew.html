Yisrael Af Al Pi Shechata — A Jew Remains a Jew, and the Covering Has a Name

27 Elul 5786 · 9 September 2026 (eve of RH 5787)

A Jew remains a Jew.

That is not sentiment. It is not a slogan for a dispatch box. It is Sanhedrin 44a: ישראל אף על פי שחטא ישראל הוא — Yisrael af al pi shechata, Yisrael hu. Even though he has sinned, he is still Israel. It is Kiddushin 68b reading Devarim 7:3–4: the child of a Jewish mother is called your child. It is Rambam in Issurei Biah. And it is the Alter Rebbe in Tanya, chapter 1: every Jew, tzaddik or rasha, carries a nefesh Elokit, a portion of G-d above. The kelipah can thicken until the spark is inaudible to the man himself. It does not leave.

That is why I will not accept “ham sandwich.”

The phrase is clever. It is also sheker. It treats Jewishness as a lifestyle brand that can be revoked when the politics turn ugly — as if the noun were a membership card issued by the Right and cancelled by the Left, or the reverse. From Yerushalayim the error is obvious in both directions. One camp wants to exile the inconvenient Jew from the people; the other wants to wear the people as a costume. Both treat the brit as optional branding. Neither is Torah.

Tell such a man he is not a Jew and — as I have written before — the neshama yells from within. The yell is not a policy preference. It is the refusal of the nefesh Elokit to be cancelled by a punchline. The work is not excommunication. The work is to keep the noun and name the covering.

Ed Miliband is a Jew because Dobra Jenta Kozak was a Jew.

The dig

She was born 22 December 1934 in Częstochowa, daughter of Dawid and Bronislawa Kozak, who owned a steel and cutlery works of some three hundred souls. Steel-town Jewish prosperity: a factory, a family name, a street that still knew who they were. The Germans seized the plant in 1939. On Yom Kippur 1942 the ghetto was liquidated — about two thousand murdered on the spot, tens of thousands sent to Treblinka. Dawid stayed behind with his frail parents, Adela and Mosiek. They were murdered. He died in January 1945 at Hailfingen after the camps. Some sixty of the extended family were destroyed. [VERIFY: The Times reporting on the Kozak / Marion Miliband family history.]

The child who became Marion was smuggled out with her mother, her sister Hadassa, and an aunt. Nuns hid them. Then Helena and Andrzej Sitkowski hid them in Warsaw. Yad Vashem would later name the Sitkowskis Righteous Among the Nations; Marion stood at that ceremony in 1996. In May 1947, age twelve, she was sent to Britain through Rabbi Solomon Schonfeld’s child-rescue work and placed with an Orthodox family near Stamford Hill. Her mother and sister later made their way toward Israel. The grandmother Ed would visit as a boy lived in Tel Aviv. There were kibbutz cousins. That is not a speechwriter’s ornament. That is the wreckage and the remnant of a real Jewish house. [VERIFY: The Telegraph on Marion’s wartime rescue and Schonfeld placement.]

I dig here on purpose. The maternal line is not a biographical flourish attached to a politics essay. It is the argument. Halakhah does not wait for synagogue attendance, party card, or self-description. Maternal Jewishness is the gate. Once through that gate, the person remains inside the people — accountable, claimable, and, in Elul language, still addressable for return. The sixty murdered relatives are not props for a sanctions brief; they are the destroyed house from which the living Jew descends. The Tel Aviv grandmother is not a closing flourish; she is the remnant that fled toward Zion. To use both as opening prayer for measures against Jews living on that land, while treating the Chief Rabbi’s alarm as disposable, is to turn memory into instrument.

Revoking the noun is the cheaper move. Digging is the harder one, because digging forces you to see both the miracle of survival and what was later done with the story.

The covering

Then came the covering.

At LSE she studied under, and in 1961 married, Ralph Miliband — Belgian-born, Polish-Jewish, Marxist, the man of Parliamentary Socialism and Socialism for a Sceptical Age, whose household on Edis Street became a New Left salon. Tony Benn ate there. Tariq Ali ate there. Joe Slovo ate there. The boys were not sent to cheder. They were invited into adult political argument. Ed would later say the family “defined themselves not by their Jewishness but by their politics,” and that he is a “Jewish atheist.” The Jewish roots, he told the Jewish Chronicle in 2010, taught him “left values” of justice. That sentence is the entire fork in the road: Sinai reduced to a mood of the Left. [VERIFY: Jewish Chronicle, 2010 interview / profile of Ed Miliband on Jewish identity and “left values.”]

Marion was not a bystander in that translation. She was an early CND activist, a strong-minded socialist and feminist, and — this is the detail the Commons rhetoric never includes — a signatory of the founding statements of Jews for Justice for Palestinians in 2002 and Independent Jewish Voices in 2007. The Jewish Chronicle already recorded this in 2010, while her sons were calling themselves “critical friends” of Israel. David said at the time it was a cause she would have to sit out. She did not sit it out. She signed. She died this past 27 May, age ninety-one. May her memory be for a blessing. The indictment is not the hidden child of Częstochowa. The indictment is what was later done with her story. [VERIFY: Jewish Chronicle on Marion Miliband’s JFJFP / IJV founding signatures and 2010 reporting.]

Name the metallurgy plainly. One Jewish house turns survival into a salon of universalism that learns to discipline the Jewish particular. Another Jewish house — mine among them, and many in Yerushalayim — treats the particular as the condition of any honest universal. Same raw material of Jewish survival. Opposite furnaces. The covering is not “having politics.” Jews have always had politics. The covering is the ideology that wears Jewish memory as vestment while teaching the children that Jewish peoplehood is the thing to be managed, educated, or overcome.

Sinai does not dissolve into “left values.” Justice is commanded — repeatedly, fiercely — but it is commanded to a people, under a Name, with obligations that bind even when the coalition does not clap. When Jewish roots are narrated primarily as an apprenticeship in progressive mood, the particular has already been melted down. What remains can still say “proud.” What remains cannot easily say hineni inside the threatened minyan.

What was done with it this week

On 8 September 2026, two days before the threshold of Rosh Hashana 5787, the Foreign Secretary opened a House of Commons statement “as a proud British Jew.” He named the sixty murdered relatives. He named the grandmother who found a home in Tel Aviv. He rejected the “erasure of Israel.” And then he announced an import ban on goods from Israeli communities over the Green Line, an embargo on arms that “materially” support those communities, and sanctions on those who finance or build there — coordinated with France, Canada, and nine other states — while accusing settlers of “ethnic cleansing.” Israel closed the British consulate in Jerusalem. [VERIFY: Jerusalem Post and primary Commons record of the 8 September 2026 statement and measures.]

The Chief Rabbi, Sir Ephraim Mirvis, called it “a truly dark day for British Jews” and said the measures would bolster those who use hatred as a weapon against Jews. The Board of Deputies and the Jewish Leadership Council warned about security and about a de facto boycott arriving in an Israeli election season. Miliband answered that he does not agree with the Chief Rabbi. [VERIFY: The Guardian and communal statements; Mirvis wording against primary text.]

Then Justin Webb asked the only question that mattered. Many British Jews will walk into synagogue this weekend feeling less safe. Will you go?

“I don’t go to synagogue.” [VERIFY: Jewish Chronicle / BBC exchange reporting.]

That is not a biographical footnote. It is the unmasking of the credential.

A man may be a Jew and never enter a shul. Halakhah does not wait at the door. But a man who invokes murdered kin and a Tel Aviv grandmother in order to sanction the living Jewish polity, who publicly overrules the recognized religious head of British Jewry, and who then announces that he does not stand in the minyan whose doors now require armed police — that man is using Jewishness as vestment. He is not standing with the covenanted people. He is standing over them with a Foreign Office brief.

This is not prophecy. Prophecy hurts from inside the people. This is domination dressed as conscience.

Covenantal republican language helps here without turning the essay into a monograph. Non-domination means no office — not even a Foreign Secretary’s — should hold arbitrary power to pick which Jews count as conscience and which Jews are the problem to be disciplined. Standing firm means a people may argue bitterly about governments and borders without accepting that their collective existence is permanently on probation. When “proud British Jew” becomes the opening prayer for measures the Chief Rabbi calls a dark day, the credential has ceased to be solidarity. It has become cover.

Notice the sequence. Memory. Rejection of “erasure.” Then the sanctions package and the ethnic-cleansing charge. Then disagreement with the Chief Rabbi. Then the refusal of the synagogue doorway. Each step alone can be spun. Together they unmask. A man may criticise Israeli policy without this choreography. What this choreography does is recruit Jewish catastrophe and Jewish refuge as moral capital for pressure on the living polity — while declining to stand with the British Jews who must walk through metal detectors because the temperature has risen. That is not prophecy from within. That is power speaking as if it were conscience.

Why the covering matters more than the sandwich

The unencumbered self — Sandel’s phrase — wants Jewishness as a portable moral license: pick it up at the dispatch box, set it down before Maariv. Thick identity does not work that way. Covenant binds the woodchopper and the water-drawer, the one who goes to shul and the one who does not, the Chief Rabbi and the factory child from Częstochowa. Atem nitzavim hayom kulchem. You are standing today, all of you.

That is why Shapiro’s sandwich empties what Tanya keeps full. The sandwich punchline treats the man as if Jewishness were a club he has already left. Tanya refuses that. The spark is covered, not revoked. Kelipah is real; so is the nefesh Elokit under it. If you tell him he is not a Jew, you have done the Left’s work of turning identity into costume — only from the opposite direction. If you keep the noun and name the covering, you have done something older and harder: you have spoken as a people that still believes in teshuvah.

I know the other path because I have spent a life walking it in the other direction: from steel-city self-reliance to the hills of Yerushalayim, from the Simple Jew who blocks hypocrites because time is short, to a family table that still makes room for the parsha. Marion’s childhood was a steel works too. Same raw material of Jewish survival. Opposite metallurgy. One house turned survival into a salon of universalism that learned to discipline the Jewish particular. The other house treats the particular as the condition of any honest universal.

The point is not to romanticise either house. Ralph Miliband’s Marxism was serious; Marion’s rescue was miraculous; Ed’s office is real power. The point is moral clarity on the threshold of a year. A credential that invokes Shoah kin to sanction the polity those kin’s survivors built — while declining the threatened doorway of the community that must live with the spillover — is not “Jewish conscience.” It is an unencumbered self wearing memory.

Elul, and the wager that remains

Elul is the season when the King is in the field. The field includes a neshama under five feet of ideology. Tanya does not say the spark is gone. It says the shell is thick. The avodah of these days is not to un-Jew a Jew. It is to refuse the sheker that a proud credential is the same thing as fidelity. The King receives without appointment; He does not confuse reception with flattery. Approach is open. Accounting remains.

A Jew remains a Jew. That is the mercy and the severity together. It means Miliband does not get to resign the noun when it becomes inconvenient, and we do not get to revoke it when we are angry. It also means the covering can, in principle, be lifted. That is not naivete. That is the whole wager of teshuvah on the threshold of a new year. Yom HaDin does not ask first whether a man liked the Chief Rabbi’s press line. It asks whether he stood with truth, with the Name, and with the people he claims when useful.

The synagogue he does not enter will still be full on Friday night. The security will still be there. The neshama he carries will still be there, under the shell, whether he names it or not. Our task, from these hills and from every intact Jewish table, is the older one: keep the noun; name the covering; leave the door of return open without pretending the covering is fidelity.

Simple distinction, old as Sinai:

Noun: Jew.

Covering: ideology.

Avodah: uncover.

Not: revoke.
