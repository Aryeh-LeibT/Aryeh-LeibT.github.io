Tehillim 25: To You I Lift My Soul

12 Nov 2026 / 2 Kislev 5787

By Aryeh Leib Thorne Toren

On 11 November 2026, the first of Kislev, I finished a 96-page study booklet on Tehillim 25, a prayer arranged on the Hebrew alphabet. After the last letter, one more verse is added, a prayer for the community: "O G-d, redeem Israel from all its distress."

The downloadable excerpt is the introduction and Chapter One. The psalm's first sentence, in the 1985 Jewish Publication Society translation, is "O L-rd, I set my hope on You." Radak reads the Hebrew as "To You, O L-rd, I lift my soul."

The Malbim calls that verse the pivot of the psalm. An archer chooses a mark and aims everything at it. The psalmist has chosen his mark: G-d. Midrash Tehillim hears the same phrase in the law of the hired labourer, who depends on his wage and must be paid that day.

The full psalm is printed at the end of the excerpt.
