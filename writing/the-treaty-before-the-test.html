The Treaty Before the Test

By Aryeh Toren_Thorne, Torah scholar, researcher and writer

On Friday 30 October 2026 the sixth aliyah of Vayera closes at Be'er Sheva. Avraham and Avimelech swear an oath, seven ewes are set apart as a witness, and two neighbors go home with an agreement. On Shabbat we read the next words: "וַיְהִי אַחַר הַדְּבָרִים הָאֵלֶּה וְהָאֱלֹקִים נִסָּה אֶת אַבְרָהָם", "And it came to pass after these things, that G-d tested Avraham."

After which things?

The Rashbam's rule

The Rashbam, Rashi's grandson, sets a rule. Wherever the Torah says "after these things," it is joined to the section right above it. He brings examples. After Avram defeated the kings, G-d told him, "Fear not." After Mordechai reported Bigtan and Teresh, Achashverosh promoted Haman, and the rescue of the king in the end saved Mordechai.

Then he applies the rule here: "אף כאן אחר הדברים שכרת אברהם ברית לאבימלך לו ולנינו ולנכדו של אברהם ונתן לו שבע כבשות הצאן וחרה אפו של הקב"ה על זאת, שהרי ארץ פלשתים ניתן לאברהם וגם ביהושע מטילים על ערי חמשת סרני פלשתים גורל בכלל גבול ישראל."

Avraham bound his son and grandson to a pact with Avimelech and gave him seven ewes, and G-d was angry. The land of the Plishtim had been given to Avraham, and in Yehoshua's day lots were cast for the cities of the five Plishti lords as part of Israel's border.

"See what your covenant was worth"

The Rashbam reads "וְהָאֱלֹקִים נִסָּה" in the same light: "קנתרו וצערו", He provoked him and pained him. Then he puts words in G-d's mouth: "נתגאיתה בבן שנתתי לך לכרות ברית ביניכם ובין בניהם. ועתה לך והעלהו לעולה וראה מה הועילה כריתות ברית שלך!"

"You took pride in the son I gave you, enough to make a covenant between you and their children. Now go and offer him up, and see what your covenant was worth."

He adds that he later found the same idea in Midrash Shmuel. G-d said, "את נתת לו שבע כבשות, חייך, שבניו עושים שבע מלחמות עם בניך ונוצחין אותן." You gave him seven ewes; by your life, his children will fight seven wars with yours and defeat them. Other versions in the same passage say seven righteous men would fall, Shimshon, Chofni and Pinchas, Shaul and his three sons; or that seven sanctuaries would be destroyed; or that the Ark would wander seven months in the field of the Plishtim.

His view, not the only view

This is a hard reading, and it is the Rashbam's own. Rashi, quoting the Gemara, hears "after these things" as after the words of the Satan or of Yishmael. The Radak ties the Akeida to the Avimelech chapter too, but as a marker of time, with no rebuke at all. A reader should hold the Rashbam's view as one strong voice in the room. Paper 4 in this set gives the others their full say.

Still, the Rashbam saw something in the text. Avimelech asked for an oath "לִי וּלְנִינִי וּלְנֶכְדִּי" (21:23), for me, my son and my grandson. Avraham said, "אָנֹכִי אִשָּׁבֵעַ", I will swear. He promised for people not yet born, over a land G-d had already promised to those same people.

The questions it leaves us

Israel has signed agreements with neighbors and weighs others today. The Rashbam does not hand us a policy, and this paper does not argue for one. He hands us questions.

Is every agreement over the land the treaty at Be'er Sheva? Or does the Torah also know agreements that are wisdom and peace, and how would we tell them apart?

Who is bound? Avraham swore for his grandson. How should one generation weigh a promise that binds the next, about land the next has its own claim to?

Where is the danger in the Rashbam's eyes: in the agreement, or in the pride he names, "נתגאיתה בבן", the feeling that our own arrangements secure our children's future?

And what does ה' ask in return? At the end of the Akeida ה' swears His own oath (22:16): "בִּי נִשְׁבַּעְתִּי נְאֻם ה'". One oath was made with a neighbor; the other came from Heaven. A people that makes agreements has to decide which of the two it leans on.

These are questions for every reader across the spectrum, and the Torah asks them before any of us do.
