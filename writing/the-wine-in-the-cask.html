The Wine in the Cask

Aryeh Leib Thorne Toren

26 November 2026 · 16 Kislev 5787 · Jerusalem · in the week of Parashas Vayishlach (Shabbat 28 November 2026)

Prepared in deep partnership and cooperation with Grok and xAI

Every strike asks the same question the morning after: who answers, and can you carry the answer?

In the days of 24–26 November the question is being asked in Riyadh.

On Tuesday, 24 November, a Houthi drone hit the tallest tower in the Saudi capital, the building that houses the Public Investment Fund, the kingdom's sovereign wealth fund. It was the second strike on the tower in less than a week; the first, on 19 November, damaged the building but caused no injuries. Saudi civil defence said 11 people were wounded in the Tuesday strike. The Saudi-led coalition fighting the Houthis promised a firm response. Qatar and Pakistan, the two mediators in the wider Iran–US war, both condemned the attack.

The response came at Hodeidah, the Houthi-held port on Yemen's Red Sea coast. On 25 November the coalition said it had targeted operational capabilities there. Al Jazeera's headline that day, drawing on AFP and Reuters, was flat: "Saudi-led coalition intercepts Houthi attacks after Hodeidah strike."

Then the answer to the answer. On Wednesday morning, 25 November, the coalition said Saudi air defences had destroyed four drones over Ras Tanura in the Eastern Province, where, as The National put it, "its largest refinery is located." The coalition spokesman, Turki Al-Maliki, said fragments fell but no casualties were reported in the first assessment. The Houthis claimed they set the refinery on fire. Reuters, and later The National, reported that satellite images from NASA and Planet Labs showed thick black smoke over the installation. The coalition did not say the refinery was hit, and as of 26 November neither Aramco nor the Saudi Energy Ministry had issued a damage report for this incident. So the interception is confirmed. The damage is not.

By Thursday, 26 November, the Houthis had claimed attacks on the Saudi Red Sea ports of Jeddah and Jazan, in retaliation for the Hodeidah strike. The coalition said a projectile caused minor damage at Jazan port, with no civilian casualties. It did not confirm that Jeddah port had been hit. That morning the coalition also said air defences had intercepted two ballistic missiles launched toward Riyadh and destroyed two drones over the Eastern Province.

That is the shape of it: a tower, a port, a refinery. And by Thursday, 26 November, a Red Sea port of Saudi Arabia's own had been added to the list.

The arithmetic Jacob did

The Torah portion for the week of 26 November is the argument after Shechem. Simeon and Levi act without asking their father. Jacob's answer is not about who was right. It is arithmetic: my fighters "are few in number, so that if they unite against me and attack me, I and my house will be destroyed." Rashi gives the picture from the Midrash: "the wine in the cask was clear but you have troubled it."

Riyadh is not few in number. But it is doing Jacob's arithmetic. A refinery, an export terminal, a tower that holds the national savings: each is an address the other side can reach. The Houthis do not need to win. They only need to keep the wine troubled.

The arithmetic runs the other way, too. Hodeidah is the port through which food reaches the people the Houthis rule. Every drone they send at Saudi oil puts their own lifeline on the list.

What ended it in the Torah

In the Torah, the danger did not end with a bigger sword. Jacob went up to Bethel and kept a vow he had left open for years, and a terror fell on the cities around them, "so that they did not pursue the sons of Jacob."

The modern version is less poetic. It is the mediators. When Qatar and Pakistan condemn a strike on a Saudi tower, they are saying the people trying to end the Iran–US war see Yemen as part of that war, not a side show.

What to watch: whether Aramco or the Saudi Energy Ministry says anything about Ras Tanura operations (smoke on a satellite image is not a damage report), and whether the Qatar–Pakistan track names Yemen out loud.

Jacob's question is the right one for every capital in the region as of 26 November. Not were we right? but who answers, and can we carry it?
