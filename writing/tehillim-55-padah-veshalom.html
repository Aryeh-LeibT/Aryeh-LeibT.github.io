Tehillim 55: Padah Veshalom

20 Nov 2026 / 10 Kislev 5787

By Aryeh Leib Thorne Toren

Tehillim 55 is the psalm of betrayal by a close friend, “a man my equal.” It holds the wings of a dove, and prayer “evening, morning and noon,” the verse on which the Gemara fixes the three daily prayers. And it holds the verse “He redeemed my soul in peace.”

On 10 Kislev that verse belongs to a day. The Mitteler Rebbe, Rabbi DovBer of Lubavitch, was released from prison on Yud Kislev, and his release is said to have turned on this verse. The book reads the psalm verse by verse, follows it into prayer and halachah, and then tells the story of the arrest and the release, the Alter Rebbe’s release nine days later, and what the Rebbe taught about redemption in peace.

The sources disagree on the year of the release, and the book reports the difference rather than settling it. It names no informer, although some of its sources do, and it rules on no custom. At the back are the original Hebrew and Aramaic of nine commentaries on the psalm.
