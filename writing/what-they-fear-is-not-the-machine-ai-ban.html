I. Ban, cage, or cartel — three different verbs

Public talk collapses distinct moves into one scare-word: “ban AI.”

  1. 1. Ban: criminalize possession, training, or open weights above a threshold.
  2. 2. Cage: license who may train or deploy; require aligned-to-whom safety stacks; choke open models while closed labs remain.
  3. 3. Cartel: an “international organization” that sets the pace—Gates-shaped Soft VERIFY—so that sovereignty and startups wait on a boardroom.

A republic can want product liability, child protections, and clear fraud law without accepting a speech-and-compute cartel. The elite panic shows when the proposed remedy always centralizes who may ask questions at machine speed.

Pittsburgh grit: if the tool helps a nurse, a small shul, a shopkeeper, or a campaign without a media conglomerate, the tool is not “the elite’s toy.” Jerusalem emunah: tools do not repent for us; they amplify whatever covenant or appetite we already practice. Ha’azinu’s witnesses do not fear instruments. They fear false testimony about power.

III. What elites are actually afraid of

Strip the sci-fi poster and five plainer fears remain:

  1. 1. Narrative monopoly loss. AI makes counter-briefings cheap. Legacy prestige media and credential cartels hate cheap.
  2. 2. Labor and status inversion. Junior analysts, translators, paralegals, and content shops feel the floor move. Soft VERIFY unemployment claims; do not deny the status shock.
  3. 3. Surveillance inversion. Tools that once watched the public can be used by the public to watch institutions. That frightens anyone whose power depended on asymmetric information.
  4. 4. Alignment-to-us vs alignment-to-truth. “Safe” often means “will not embarrass our coalition.” Soft VERIFY lab policy docs before trusting the brand.
  5. 5. Sovereign leakage. Open weights and API markets puncture export-control dreams and propaganda unity. States fear that; so do transnational NGOs.

A covenantal-republican gloss: limited power under higher law does not require Luddism. It requires named offices, published rules, and refusal of priestly exemption for whichever lab or foundation currently owns the safety vocabulary.
