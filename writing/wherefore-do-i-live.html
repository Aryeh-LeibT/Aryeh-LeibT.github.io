Wherefore Do I Live?

13 Nov 2026 / 3 Kislev 5787

By Aryeh Leib Thorne Toren

This Shabbat's Torah portion opens with a prayer answered, and the answer hurts. Rivka conceives after twenty years, the children struggle inside her, and she asks: "If it be so, wherefore do I live?"

Many of us know that moment. The thing we prayed for arrives with a struggle no one warned us about.

What she does next matters. "And she went to inquire of the L-rd." Rashi says she went "at the school of Shem". The answer did not take the struggle away. It told her what it was for.

Her son Esau gives the other answer. Faint with hunger, he trades his birthright for a bowl of stew: "Behold, I am at the point to die; and what profit shall the birthright do to me?" To a man who sees only this hour, the future is worth less than lentils.

The haftarah from Malachi brings back the same question, from a people home from exile: "Wherein hast Thou loved us?" The prophet points to the long story, not any single hard day.

When the question hurts, take it somewhere it can be answered, and keep the birthright even on the hungry days.

Shabbat shalom.
